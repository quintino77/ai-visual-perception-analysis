Can you tell whether a face is real—or AI-generated?

An interesting question is how that ability relates to age. But the evidence also challenges a popular assumption: that AI images were easy to identify back in 2022.

Nightingale and Farid's 2022 experiment reported just 48.2% accuracy when participants classified real and synthetic faces—close to chance.

A 2026 preprint by Kim and Kim adds another dimension: among 1,664 adults aged twenty to sixty-nine, accuracy was negatively associated with age, and the association was stronger on mobile devices.

The chart translates the reported slopes into percentage points per ten-year age difference. It describes differences between participants, not how an individual's ability changes over time.

These studies cannot be combined into a simple 2022–2026 decline: the images, participants and methods differ. They also do not show that everyone is now unable to recognize synthetic media.

My takeaway for AI evaluation: a useful benchmark should examine who is making the judgment, what they are seeing and under which conditions. One average score can hide important differences.
